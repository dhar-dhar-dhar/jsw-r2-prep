# Talk track, ten numbers and drill (private; written 5 Oct 2026, 16:45 IST, corrected 17:30)

## The bet in two sentences
In India the state sets the toll on distribution (UPI MDR back at 0.4 percent above Rs 2,000 from 15 Oct; IRDAI proposing commission caps on 23 Sep) and licensed players at scale collect it. Durable margin sits with whoever holds the risk on a licensed book and runs the operation with software; the AI test is the same from the other side: software earns the margin only where it holds the outcome.

## Five-question drill
1. Who actually gets the 0.4 percent? Not published. On Rs 10,000 the fee is Rs 40; an acquirer keeps an assumed 30 to 50 percent, Rs 12 to 20; on more than 95 percent of merchant transactions nothing. Payments is a wedge for software.
2. Your lender loses money for three years. Why fund it? Year-one ROA of minus 3 to minus 10 percent is the cost of building a book. The test is cost of funds under 12 percent and opex under 4 percent of assets by year three, with the first Rs 100 cr of debt contracted at entry. Aye and Moneyview show the IPO pays for profit.
3. IRDAI is capping commissions. Is distribution finished? PB Fintech still earned Rs 163 cr in Q1 FY27. First-year commission (Rs 3,000 to 4,000 on a Rs 20,000 policy) became a regulated toll on 23 Sep. Back the claims line (Rs 50 to 300 per claim against the insurer's Rs 500 to 2,000) and the carriers (health premiums up 29.7 percent).
4. Is voice AI not just efficiency? Rs 2 to 6 of cost against Rs 8 to 25 of human cost per call is efficiency, and India's cheap calls leave a price room of Rs 5 to 12 per resolved call. It becomes something else only when the company carries the outcome. SquadStack's success fee is a vendor's price; DMI Finance holds the loss.
5. Where does the fraud budget come from? Three dated rules: bank liability from 1 Jan 2027, DPDP from 13 Nov 2026 and 13 May 2027, the mule-account procedure. Fewer than 200 buyers, only 20.8 percent using AI in 2025, so the budget needs a rule.

## Twelve counters he may raise, one-line answers
1. Which of these would Peak XV argue with? The bet: most funds back the distributor; I back the risk-holder and name what that rules out.
2. Lenders at 2 to 3x book cannot return a fund. The fund described needs six outcomes at 5 to 8x and no zeros; Rs 1,350 cr back on Rs 450 cr.
3. Collection cost is branches, and Five Star owns them. I only back lenders where repayment is controlled by the rail.
4. The unsecured clean-out is over; why avoid? Avoid new entrants; the survivors are the acquirers and exits (Moneyview, Kissht, Fibe).
5. Bima Sugam and FDI help insurers, not distributors. Agreed; the insurance call backs manufacturing and infrastructure.
6. Who has made money on advice in India? Nobody found at scale; the wealth call backs manufacturers with captive distribution.
7. Per-outcome collections has existed for thirty years. A contract is not a risk-holder; hold the loss.
8. Why does HDFC Bank not serve the Rs 20,000 borrower first? It cannot cannibalise its branches or its cost base; where there is no structural reason I do not invest.
9. GST and AA data reach every lender. The data is a commons; the edge is the model trained on the company's own losses through a cycle, and the licence to act on it.
10. Who pays over Rs 1,000 cr for these in 2031? The public market, on profit after tax.
11. Bessemer and Stellaris already say outcome-priced AI services. Direction agreed, stopping point not; a success fee is a vendor's price.
12. MDR is back; does payments reopen? The toll goes to licensed acquirers at scale; the Rs 300 cap keeps the margin in Rs 2,000 to 75,000 tickets, a wedge for vertical software.
