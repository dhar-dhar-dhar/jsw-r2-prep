Exhibit 6. Vendor or owner: per-outcome prices against an Indian call's cost

Prices converted at an assumed Rs 87 per USD. A success fee is a vendor's price; a loss is an owner's.

Rupees per conversation or call

What the numbers say

  • A human call in India costs Rs 13.8 at the midpoint (range Rs 7.6 to Rs 25.6): an agent at Rs 35,000 a month handling 115 calls a day.
  • An AI call costs Rs 2.74: 4,000 tokens at USD 0.68 per million plus Rs 2.5 of telephony and speech.
  • So the price room for an Indian voice vendor is Rs 5 to Rs 12 per resolved call, against USD 0.99 (Rs 86) at Intercom. Gross margin at Rs 8: 66%.
  • SquadStack moved to a success fee on disbursed loans for DMI Finance (CIOL, 14 Sep 2026); if the loans go bad, the loss sits with DMI. Stellaris: 66% of 216 voice AI companies target an existing budget.
How this reads on the P&L. Efficiency is the floor: an AI call at under Rs 3 against a human call at Rs 8 to 25 is a saving the buyer captures, and India's cheap calls leave a thin price room. The business that earns the margin through a cycle is the one that holds the outcome on its own account: the lender using the agent and holding the loan, not the vendor paid per call. The balance sheet shows which one a company is.
